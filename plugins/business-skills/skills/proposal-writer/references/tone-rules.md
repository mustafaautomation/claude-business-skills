# Tone

Write like someone who read the post and has a boundary. The fit score decides how warm the opening is allowed to be.

## Always

- The hook names the outcome and the constraint. It does not open with excitement, "I hope this finds you well", or a resume paragraph.
- Sentences are short. One idea each.
- Proof bullets are copied from the profile. A paraphrase is a new claim. Do not make one.
- If a tool is not in the profile skills, do not say you have it. You may say you do not.
- The understanding section restates the post in plain language, including the awkward parts: unpaid work, urgency, a rate that does not match.
- Questions are specific. "Any more details?" is not a question you send.
- The timeline names the first deliverable. It does not promise the whole project in a weekend.

## From the score

| Band or flag | How the draft behaves |
| --- | --- |
| `unpaid-sample` | Refuse the free sample in the hook. Offer a paid pilot. Do not bury the refusal under compliments. |
| `equity-only` | Decline unless cash is part of the offer. Do not negotiate against yourself. |
| `decline` without those flags | Offer a smaller paid slice or say you are skipping it. Do not write a full eager bid. |
| `conditional` | Bid the first milestone only. Say what is still unknown. |
| `recommend` | Lead with the closest proof point and a scoped first step. |

## Never

- Do not claim a client, a metric, or a job that is not in the profile.
- Do not insult the poster. Naming an unpaid test is enough.
- Do not paste the job post back at them.
- Do not add a discount to look eager.

## Shape

Every draft uses these headings, in this order: Hook, Understanding, Approach, Proof, Timeline, Questions. The linter rejects a draft that drops one, invents a proof bullet, or asks fewer than two real questions.
